title: Act One

## Scene 1

(Evening. Rain against the window. ELIAS is at the bench with his back to the door, a type bar in a pair of tweezers. The BELL rings. MARA enters, shaking out an umbrella, carrying a green typewriter case.)

MARA
Are you still open?

ELIAS
(without turning)
That depends on what's in the case.

MARA
My father's typewriter.

(ELIAS sets down the tweezers and turns. He looks at the case for a long moment before he looks at her.)

ELIAS
Then I'm still open.

(She sets the case on the counter. He opens it. Neither speaks. He presses a key. Nothing happens.)

MARA
It stopped the week he died. I know how that sounds.

ELIAS
It sounds like a typewriter. They do that. They wait.

MARA
For what?

ELIAS
(lifting the machine out, gently)
For someone to ask them what's wrong.

(The BELL rings, though no one has come in. They both look at the door.)

MARA
Does it always do that?

ELIAS
Only when it agrees with me.

(Lights fade on ELIAS turning the platen knob, listening.)

## Scene 2

(Two weeks later. Morning light. The machine sits on the counter with a sheet of paper rolled in. MARA enters. The BELL rings.)

ELIAS
Go on.

(She sits. She types. The carriage moves; the bell at the end of the line rings once, small and certain. She stops with her hands still on the keys.)

MARA
(quietly)
That's it. That's the sound.

ELIAS
It's the sound it makes when it's well. Which isn't quite the same thing.

MARA
But he'd know it.

ELIAS
He'd know it.

(Blackout.)
